FlowSafe / Dresser Utility Solutions

Facility Material Flow & Layout Analysis

Two years of operational data and a scaled AutoCAD facility model, turned into a quantified case for how FlowSafe's production, cleaning, testing, and storage areas should be arranged.

See Experience Download resume

Role: Industrial Engineering Intern  ·  FlowSafe / Dresser Utility Solutions  ·  Continuing during the academic semester

Problem

FlowSafe's facility layout — how production, cleaning, testing, and point-of-use storage areas sit relative to one another — had developed over time without a data-driven relayout. No one had a quantified picture of how much material and people movement the existing layout actually required, so there was no basis for saying which layout changes would meaningfully help versus which would just move the problem around.

Why it mattered

Every job that moves through the facility pays a travel cost between areas — production to cleaning, cleaning to testing, testing to storage, and back — and that cost repeats on every job, every day. Without a quantified baseline, layout decisions were effectively guesses. Establishing a real, data-backed measure of transportation effort was the precondition for making a defensible case for any layout change at all.

Constraints

  • Had to work within FlowSafe's existing building footprint — this is a relayout study, not new construction.
  • Had to preserve required adjacencies between production, cleaning, and testing rather than optimize travel distance in isolation.
  • Recommendations needed to be justified by a quantified model, not intuition, to be actionable for management.
  • Needed to hold up against two years of real operational variation, not a single snapshot in time.

My role

I analyzed the operational data, built the scaled AutoCAD facility model, and developed the transportation-effort analysis independently as my project during my Industrial Engineering internship at FlowSafe / Dresser Utility Solutions.

Data & engineering approach

  • Built a scaled AutoCAD model of the facility covering 104 distinct activity locations.
  • Constructed the full set of origin-destination travel paths between those locations from two years of production/operational data — 10,816 paths in total.
  • Applied a distance × movement-frequency transportation-effort model to quantify how much effort the current layout requires, giving a current-state baseline to test future layouts against.
  • Used the Valve Decoder database to close data gaps in the historical valve-shipment records feeding this analysis.

Development process

I built the current-state model first — mapping all 104 activity locations and calculating baseline transportation effort from the historical travel-path data — then iterated on layout scenarios, testing changes to production-area placement, cleaning-area placement, testing-capacity allocation, and point-of-use storage locations, recalculating modeled transportation effort for each scenario until arriving at a recommended future-state layout.

Solution

A recommended future-state facility layout with specific changes to production-area organization, cleaning-area placement, testing-capacity allocation, and point-of-use storage locations — every recommendation justified by the same distance × frequency effort model used to establish the current-state baseline, so the comparison between current and future state is apples-to-apples.

Quantified result

18.75%
Reduction in modeled transportation effort, current state vs. recommended future state
~1 year
Planning-level payback for the recommended changes

18.75% is a reduction in modeled transportation effort (distance × movement frequency) — not a direct cost or dollar-value reduction. Payback is a planning-level estimate, not an audited figure.
